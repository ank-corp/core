title: A corpus is addressed by path, registered with its root commit, and arbitrated by one hub

constraint: |
  A corpus is keyed by <org>/<corpus>; its root commit is recorded at registration as a check, never as the key; and a corpus's refs/ank/* has exactly one authoritative hub.
schema: 4
version: 1

ank identifies a corpus by its root commit. Two repositories created from one
template share a root commit and are not one corpus; a fork shares it and is
one corpus for coordination purposes (fork contributors claim on the same hub,
phase 2). So the root commit is the right check and the wrong key: the hub keys
on the path, records the root commit when the corpus is registered, and refuses
a registration that names a root commit another corpus of the same organisation
already carries.

One authoritative hub per corpus is what makes compare-and-swap mean something.
Federation is reading across hubs, read-only; gossip or CRDTs over claims would
break "one winner" and are rejected.
